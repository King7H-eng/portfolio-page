## Executive summary

Three applied statistics problems in MATLAB:
1. **Regression:** predicting the compressive strength of concrete at different curing times
2. **Statistics:** analysing a wind turbine's power curve over two time periods from SCADA data
3. **Bayes:** applying Bayes' theorem to a binary symmetric channel to decide which bit was most likely sent

**Results:**
- The binder regression model performed well (training R² 0.78, test R² 0.41 on transformed data). The cement model did not: its test R² of −0.27 meant predicting the mean would have done better.
- Dataset B's 95% confidence intervals were up to 205 kWh/10 min wide, against 8.94 for dataset A. That pointed to SCADA or turbine faults, or turbulent conditions, with a second plateau suggesting deliberate curtailment.
- Posterior probabilities were used to locate the MAP decision boundaries.

## Context

The report applies the ENGI2211 learning outcomes to three common engineering applications: concrete strength in civil engineering, wind-turbine behaviour, and the binary symmetric channel as the simplest communications model.

## Methodology

**1. Regression: concrete dataset**
- Split the data by curing age: ages with more than 50 samples for training (944 points), the rest for testing (86 points)
- Took the natural log of compressive strength to linearise the data
- Calculated water:binder and water:cement ratios
- Fitted ln(strength) = b₀ + b₁ × ratio at each age, then regressed b₀ and b₁ against age to predict untrained ages
- Evaluated with **residual KDE plots** (checking for systematic bias) and **R²** on transformed and raw data, for both training and test sets

**2. Statistics: wind-turbine SCADA data, 2 × 5,000 points**
- Plotted power curves for both periods and estimated rated output (~280 kWh/10 min) and cut-in speed (~2.5 m/s). The cut-out speed was given as 25 m/s.
- Binned wind speed into 1 m/s bins and computed the mean, standard deviation and **95% confidence interval** for each bin
- Compared the two periods and set out possible explanations: SCADA errors, turbine faults, erratic weather or curtailment

**3. Bayesian analysis: binary symmetric channel**
- Computed the probability of receiving a 0 or a 1 across prior probabilities (p) and correct rates (q)
- Computed posterior probabilities with Bayes' theorem
- Located the **MAP decision boundaries** where the posterior curves intersect

## Results

- **Binder vs cement model:** the binder model was accurate on both transformed and raw data; the cement model had negative test R². Test residuals had a negative mean in both cases, meaning a systematic bias towards over-prediction.
- **Turbine data:** dataset A closely followed an ideal power curve. Dataset B showed many zero readings at rated speed, much wider confidence intervals (largest 205 vs 8.94 kWh/10 min) and a second plateau consistent with curtailment.
- **Channel:** a higher correct rate q makes a MAP decision more likely to be right. At q = 0.5, the channel is pure noise.
