## Executive summary

The aim was an easy-to-launch, efficient, low-noise, long-range aerial platform for surveying conservation areas with minimal user input. Our five-person team built a 3D-printed Eclipson fixed-wing eVTOL in a tail-sitter configuration, which takes off vertically and cruises as a fixed-wing aircraft. We set it up on ArduPilot with Mission Planner and wrote Lua scripts for an autonomous patrol mission, tested in simulation.

Structural limitations stopped the drone reaching fixed-wing cruise. Weakness between the wings and fuselage made it hard to land without significant damage, and reprinting a whole wing caused long delays. In testing, we also hit hardware, GPS calibration and electromagnetic interference issues. The report closes with recommendations to improve the drone's strength, feasibility and performance.

## Context

Traditional reserve monitoring (ranger patrols, helicopters) is expensive, slow and limited in coverage. Quadcopters have short flight times and range. Fixed-wing eVTOLs combine vertical take-off with long-range, low-noise, efficient cruise, often 2–3× the flight time of a quadcopter.

## Methodology

**1. Business case.** Set out the problem for conservation teams and the advantages of eVTOLs over quadcopters and fixed-wing aircraft. Also covered other uses: river water sampling, package delivery, powerline inspection.

**2. Airframe build**
- 3D-printed parts assembled with cyanoacrylate glue, following Eclipson's instructions
- Carbon rods in the fuselage and vertical stabiliser
- Elevons driven by servos
- Filament cost £10.50; bill of materials compiled for all components (Table I)

**3. Hardware integration**
- Holybro Pixhawk 6C Mini flight controller
- Power distribution board fed through a splitter from the LiPo battery
- Two ESCs taking PWM throttle signals
- Buck converter powering the servo rail
- GPS over UART, telemetry radio and RC receiver

**4. Ground control.** Mission Planner on ArduPilot, with patrol routes programmed as GPS waypoints.

**5. Flight testing**
- Ground checks in manual mode (flaps, trim, RC inputs) with full logging enabled
- Two flight attempts, then crash analysis from footage and logs

**6. Proposed testing** (planned but not reached):
- **Hover tuning:** FFT vibration analysis to set notch filters, then Quik Tune and AutoTune
- **Transition testing:** time and altitude change
- **Efficiency testing:** hover-only vs transition flights on a full battery, to test Eclipson's claim of triple the distance
- **Repeatability testing:** RMS error of flight paths against the programmed route

**7. Lua scripting** (Mission Planner simulation, ArduPlane with Q_ENABLE)
- Automated pre-arm checks, then arming and take-off to a set altitude
- Hover-to-FBWA transition, with tail-sit angle and angular rates tuned by experiment
- Lawnmower-style passes between four waypoints in Kruger National Park, with a loop counter, then return to home
- Noted limitation: simulation used pre-tuned PIDs, so real flight would need a quick-tune script

**8. Critical review.** Reviewed the structure, the hardware set-up and the build process, considered alternative eVTOL configurations, and made recommendations.

## Results

- **Flight 1:** the wire feet got stuck in grass and moss, and the drone pivoted over (undamaged)
- **Flight 2:** taking off from a metal plate, the drone flipped backwards and sideways on throttle-up, damaging the wings beyond repair. The logs didn't capture desired pitch, so that couldn't be ruled out as the cause.
- **Design flaws identified:**
  - Wing–fuselage joint glued with minimal overlap and no spar
  - Single-wall-loop prints
  - Thin landing legs
  - The supplied hardware differed from the design: a separate flight controller and power board, which made level mounting difficult, and a larger battery, which affected weight distribution
  - High-current wires close to the flight controller and GPS, likely causing accelerometer calibration errors
- **Process issues:**
  - Glued parts meant one break required reprinting a whole wing (£1.80 per wing)
  - Limited printer access
  - First-layer adhesion problems
  - The airstrip was a 20-minute drive away
  - Weather grounded the drone on several days
- **Recommendations:**
  - Carbon-fibre composites and rods through the wing–fuselage joint
  - A modular airframe
  - More rigid legs
  - LiDAR, optical flow and redundant barometric sensors, plus imaging
  - Smart battery management
  - For a complete redesign, a conventional VTOL with multi-rotor lift and a dedicated cruise motor
- **Future development:** live video via a VTX or digital link, and a Python/OpenCV program to detect poachers and alert authorities with their location
