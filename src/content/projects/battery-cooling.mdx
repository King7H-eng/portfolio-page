## About this project

A final-year BEng capstone dissertation, continuing the work of a previous student. It is a computational study of a new type of passive cooling for batteries, using metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) for adsorption cooling.

The project includes a literature review of the current state of battery thermal management technologies. It then involved building a model of a MOF-coated battery cell, validating it against experimental results, and scaling it up to model multiple cells in an air-cooled battery pack. Finally, it included the optimisation of a proposed design for a hybrid battery thermal management system (BTMS).

Supervised by Dr Zhiwei Ma. Builds on Lukas Koehne-Mitchell's original experimental study.

## Abstract

Battery Thermal Management Systems (BTMS) are essential for ensuring optimal performance of batteries and preventing thermal runaway. Metal-Organic Framework (MOF) coatings have shown initial promise as passive BTMS as they can redistribute heat through sorption without additional energy but their behaviour at a pack scale remains uncharacterised. This paper numerically investigates a hybrid BTMS using MOF-801 as a passive cooling coating on each cell in an air-cooled pack. A MATLAB model of an 18650 cell coated in MOF-801 was validated with experimental data and expanded to a 15 cell string under air cooling. Compared to the uncoated air-cooled base case at 0.25 m/s the MOF coated BTMS reduced the peak temperature rise of the cells by 30.5% and achieved an improvement of 43.2% in temperature uniformity in the string, at the cost of a 68.7% increase in time taken to cool back down to ambient. MOF layers on cells closer to the outlet of the airstream desorb more strongly, redistributing up to 1.7× more heat than at MOF layers on cells at the inlet, counteracting the thermal gradient created by air-cooling. Above 1 m/s, the low thermal conductivity of MOF-801 negates its sorption-cooling benefit, and at low air velocities (0.05 m/s) the airstream became saturated with moisture, limiting further desorption. The results establish pack level operating envelopes for MOF-801 in BTMS and demonstrate the coupled behaviour of passive cooling MOF layers through the air stream at pack level.
